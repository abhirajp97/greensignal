**Ideal Customer Profile · How They Operate · What They Actually Feel · How to Reach Them**

## The Most Important Thing to Understand

Small specialty coffee roasters did not get into this business because they love margins, supply chains, or commodity markets. They got into it because they love coffee — the craft, the origin stories, the ritual, the community, the sensory experience of tasting something exceptional and understanding why it tastes that way.

This matters enormously for how GreenSignal is positioned and sold. A product that leads with r-values, stocks-to-use ratios, and ENSO correlations will be ignored — not because roasters are unsophisticated, but because none of those words live in their world. A product that says *"we help you stop getting blindsided by price spikes so you can focus on the coffee"* will get a meeting.

> **The job of GreenSignal is not to turn roasters into commodity traders. It is to give roasters — who are already running a business — the same protection that large roasters have always had, in language they actually use.**

## 1. Who the ICP Is

### Demographics & Business Profile

| Characteristic | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Business size** | Owner-operated roastery. 1–5 full-time staff. Sometimes the owner is also the head roaster, sales person, and social media manager. |
| **Volume** | 100–500 kg of green coffee per month. Small enough to lack procurement infrastructure, large enough that purchasing decisions move the P&L. |
| **Revenue** | ~$300K–$2M annual revenue. Tight margins (10–20% net). A single bad purchasing decision can meaningfully hurt the year. |
| **Coffee focus** | Specialty coffee. Single-origin lots, often direct trade or through curated importers. Deep care about provenance. |
| **Age & background** | Typically 28–45. Former baristas, cafe managers, or coffee enthusiasts who "went too deep." Some have business backgrounds, most do not. |
| **Geography** | Concentrated in specialty cities: Portland, Seattle, San Francisco, Brooklyn, Chicago, Austin, Denver, Nashville, LA. Growing in second-tier markets. |
| **Online presence** | Active on Instagram — usually the owner's personal voice. Posts about new arrivals, origin trips, roasting experiments, behind-the-scenes. |
| **Existing software** | Cropster or Artisan for roasting. Shopify or Square for retail. Google Sheets for almost everything else. |

## 2. Why They Got Into Roasting

Almost none of them started a roastery because they saw a gap in the specialty coffee market. They started because:

- They fell in love with a specific cup of coffee and needed to understand it more deeply
- They had been a barista or cafe owner and wanted to control more of the process
- They were obsessed with the craft dimension — the chemistry of roasting, the terroir of different origins, the ritual of cupping
- They wanted to build a business that expressed their values: farmer relationships, transparency, quality over volume
- They wanted independence — to be their own boss, do things the right way, not compromise on quality for margin

> **What this means for GreenSignal:** the roaster's identity is tied to craft and mission. The business side — purchasing, margins, pricing — is something they tolerate, not something they love. The product that wins is the one that handles the business anxiety so they can focus on the coffee.

### What They Are Proud Of

- The specific farms and cooperatives they work with — they know the farmer's name, the altitude, the processing method
- Having discovered a new origin or lot before it became widely known
- A roast profile dialled in perfectly for a particular coffee
- Loyal wholesale accounts — the cafe that's been buying from them for four years
- Their Instagram — it reflects their taste, voice, and values
- Quality: they cup everything. A bad batch that gets through is a source of genuine shame.

### What They Are Embarrassed By

- Not knowing what's happening in the green coffee market — feeling like they're always learning about price movements after the fact
- Having to suddenly raise prices on a loyal wholesale customer
- Getting advice from their broker and not knowing if it's genuine or a sales pitch
- Running out of a popular coffee because they didn't buy enough forward
- Not being able to explain to customers why their price went up 20%

## 3. A Week in Their Life

| Time | Activity | What They're Thinking |
|------|----------|-----------------------|
| Mon morning | Roasting schedule review | Which lots are running low? Do I have enough Ethiopia for the Friday wholesale order? |
| Mon–Wed | Roasting batches | Is this roast tracking where I want it? The Huila is reading differently today — humidity? |
| Wed afternoon | Cupping session — evaluating a new green coffee sample | Is this worth buying? At $5.20/lb can I still price it attractively? |
| Thu | Order fulfilment, customer emails, social media | Need to post about the new Guatemala. Three DMs asking if the Yirgacheffe is back in stock. |
| Thu–Fri | **Green coffee purchasing decision (often ad hoc)** | My Ethiopia is down to 2 months of stock. Do I order now? Prices look high. Is this temporary? |
| Fri | Wholesale delivery, cafe check-ins | The cafe on 5th raised a question about pricing. Can I hold my prices another quarter? |
| Weekend | Markets, events, personal coffee ritual | This is why I do this. |

> **Thursday–Friday is the moment GreenSignal serves.** It is currently made on gut feel, the importer's advice (conflicted), whatever news they can find, and what they can afford this month. It takes 30–90 minutes of anxiety and still feels uncertain. GreenSignal makes that decision take 5 minutes and feel informed.

## 4. How Purchasing Decisions Work Today

### Step 1: The Trigger

Something prompts the roaster to think about buying more green coffee:
- Stock is running low on a popular coffee — they notice at 6–8 weeks of supply remaining
- An importer calls with a new offering or availability alert
- They see a post about price movements in industry press
- A peer roaster mentions they just bought forward because they're worried about prices

### Step 2: The Information Gathering (informal)

Sources, in rough order of trust:
1. Their primary green coffee importer or broker — most trusted, but has a conflict of interest
2. Perfect Daily Grind or Sprudge — industry press covering supply stories
3. Peers in the roasting community — DMs, Slack groups, industry events
4. Their own memory of what prices were 6–12 months ago (often inaccurate)
5. Occasionally: a quick Google of "coffee futures price" — gives them a number they don't know how to contextualise

### Step 3: The Decision

Made on incomplete, conflicted, or contextless information:
- *"My broker says buy now, so I'll buy now"* — relying on the relationship
- *"Prices look high, I'll wait and see"* — often the wrong call if supply is structurally tight
- *"I can only afford X bags this month anyway"* — cash flow overrides strategy
- *"I'll buy what I need for 6 weeks and reassess"* — perpetual short-term buying

> **The fundamental problem:** the roaster has no independent view of whether "now" is a good time to buy. They are entirely dependent on their importer's word, which is not neutral. GreenSignal is the first tool to give them a broker-independent perspective at a price they can afford.

### Step 4: The Aftermath

- If they bought and prices rise: relief, "I got lucky" — rarely attributed to good analysis
- If they waited and prices rise: stress, margin compression, possible customer price increase
- If they bought and prices fall: frustration with the broker
- If they held and prices fall: vindication, but the memory fades — next cycle they're uncertain again

**There is no learning loop.** Each purchasing decision is made in isolation. The same mistakes repeat across market cycles.

## 5. The Emotional Reality

Numbers justify a purchase. Emotions drive it. GreenSignal needs to address both — but it starts with the emotional reality, because that is what creates urgency.

| Feeling | When It Happens | How GreenSignal Addresses It |
|---------|-----------------|------------------------------|
| **Anxiety before a purchasing decision** | Every time they need to buy green coffee and don't know if now is a good time | Replaces uncertainty with a clear, sourced, explainable recommendation in 2 minutes |
| **Guilt when raising prices** | After a supply spike forces them to raise wholesale prices on loyal customers | Forward-buying signals give advance warning — prices can be raised gradually, not abruptly |
| **Distrust of broker advice** | Every time their importer says "buy now" and they wonder if it's genuine | Gives them an independent data point to cross-reference |
| **Fear of being caught flat-footed** | When they read about a price spike and realise they're exposed | Spike risk alerts flag supply tightening 3–12 months before it hits prices |
| **Imposter syndrome about the business side** | When peers talk about forward contracts and they feel out of their depth | Translates the complex into plain language — no jargon needed |
| **Resentment of the asymmetry** | When they realise large roasters have procurement teams and they have a spreadsheet | Explicitly positions GreenSignal as giving small roasters what large ones have always had |

## 6. Product Positioning — In Their Language

### What Works

| Instead of… | Say… | Why it works |
|-------------|------|--------------|
| "2.2% timing improvement from our ML model" | "Stop guessing when to buy. Know if today is a good time." | Removes the anxiety they actually feel |
| "Stocks-to-use ratio at 13% — multi-decade low" | "The world's coffee buffer stock is the lowest it's been in 20 years. Prices can spike fast from here." | Same information, their framing |
| "ENSO ONI lag correlation r = −0.30 at 24 months" | "La Niña is developing. Vietnam's harvest could be short in 18 months. Here's what that means for your Robusta pricing." | Specific, forward-looking, actionable |
| "Our composite signal combines 5 data sources" | "We watch the weather in Brazil, the warehouses in New York, the hedge funds' positions, and the harvest reports from every major origin — so you don't have to." | Makes the work visible, not the methodology |
| "ROI of 2.6× subscription cost in spike scenarios" | "One good decision during the 2024 price spike covered years of subscription. A 200kg roaster who bought forward in mid-2024 saved $3,000 in a single quarter." | Concrete, recent, real |
| "Broker-independent market intelligence" | "The first green coffee signal that has no interest in selling you coffee." | Directly names the distrust they already feel |

## 7. Objections You Will Hear

| Objection | What They Actually Mean | How to Respond |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| "I trust my importer — we have a great relationship" | The relationship is real. They're not saying the importer is dishonest. | "GreenSignal isn't a replacement for that relationship — it's a second opinion. When your importer says buy now, wouldn't it be nice to check against an independent signal in 30 seconds?" |
| "I can't afford another subscription right now" | Cash flow is tight, or they're not convinced the ROI is there. | "At $49/month, one good forward-buying decision before a price move covers the annual subscription. Not a prediction — just context that large roasters already have." |
| "I don't really understand futures markets" | They're worried about a learning curve. | "You don't need to. The output is a weekly signal: green/amber/red for each origin, with one paragraph of plain language. If it says Buy on the Ethiopia, it tells you why." |
| "My volumes are too small for this to matter" | They think this is for big roasters. | "A 100kg roaster who bought 3 months forward before the 2024 spike saved $1,500 in one quarter — 2.6× the annual subscription." |
| "How do I know your signals are accurate?" | Trust is the real issue. | "They're not price predictions. They're context: where current prices sit in their historical range, what USDA says about global supply, what the weather has been doing in Minas Gerais. Every number is linkable to its source." |
| "I already use Cropster" | They think this might overlap with Cropster. | "Cropster is for after you've bought the coffee — roast profiles, batch management. GreenSignal is for the moment before: should you buy, how much, which origins are risky right now. Cropster doesn't touch that." |

## 8. How to Reach Them

### Most Effective Channels (in order)

| Channel | Approach | What Works | What Doesn't |
|---------|----------|------------|--------------|
| **Instagram DM** | Find roasters whose owners post personally about sourcing and pricing anxiety. DM as a peer, not a vendor. | Reference something specific about their coffee. "I saw you posted about price pressure on your Ethiopia — that's exactly the problem I'm building around." | Generic pitches. Anything that sounds like a template. |
| **Cold email** | Owner's email via website or LinkedIn. Under 100 words. Lead with the problem, not the product. | Specificity about the 2024/25 price situation. A question, not a pitch. | Long emails. Feature lists. |
| **SCA events** | World of Coffee, SCA Expo, regional events. Show up, be curious, don't pitch. | Genuine curiosity about their sourcing process. Sharing what you're building as something you're exploring. | Hard pitching at events. |
| **Roast Magazine** | Publishes annual Micro/Small Roaster of the Year lists — exact ICP. Also accepts contributed articles. | Write a genuine piece about green coffee market intelligence for small roasters. Positions you as a domain expert. | Advertorial tone. |
| **r/roasting (Reddit)** | Occasional threads about pricing and green coffee sourcing. | Contribute genuinely to pricing/sourcing discussions over time before mentioning the product. | Announcing the product without having contributed first. |
| **Coffee Roasters Alliance (Facebook)** | Active group. Green coffee pricing comes up regularly during spikes. | Share data insights organically. "I was looking at USDA supply data this week and..." | Promotional posts. |

## 9. What the Product Must Feel Like

| Design Principle | What It Means Concretely | What to Avoid |
|------------------|--------------------------|---------------|
| **Plain language first** | Every signal has a one-paragraph human explanation. Not "composite score: 0.28". Instead: "Ethiopia Yirgacheffe is near a 2-year price low. Global supply is tight. La Niña is developing. This looks like a good window to buy." | Any unexplained number, acronym, or technical term visible without a toggle to "see the data" |
| **Calibrated honesty** | Never says "prices will rise". Says "leading indicators suggest upward pressure — moderate confidence". A wrong confident prediction destroys trust permanently. | Confident price predictions. Precise target prices. Anything that sounds like a trading signal. |
| **Respects their expertise** | The roaster knows coffee better than GreenSignal ever will. The product helps with market timing, not quality or sourcing ethics. | Any suggestion that the product knows better about coffee quality or origin selection. |
| **Fast to the insight** | A roaster checks the signal in 2 minutes on Thursday before calling their importer. Not a research dashboard. | Dense dashboards. Too many charts. Anything requiring 15 minutes to extract the recommendation. |
| **Independent and trustworthy** | Data sources are named and linkable. "We pulled this from USDA's monthly supply report — here's the link." | Black-box recommendations with no visible sourcing. |
| **No startup aesthetic** | The specialty coffee world values craft and authenticity. The product should feel like it was built by someone who understands coffee. | Generic SaaS design. Corporate blue. Anything that looks like it was built for enterprise procurement. |

### The Onboarding Question That Changes Everything

The first thing GreenSignal should ask a new user is not "what origins do you buy?" It is:

> *"Has a green coffee price spike ever caught you off guard — and what did it cost you?"*

Let them tell their story. Almost every roaster over 3 years has one. That story is why they will pay for GreenSignal. The origins, volumes, and importers come after. The emotional anchor comes first.
